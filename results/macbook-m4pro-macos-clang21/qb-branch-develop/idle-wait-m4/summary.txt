idle-wait-m4 -- what an idle poll of a peer's line costs on Apple Silicon, by primitive (Huly QB-181, the arm64 half).
Instrument: tools/probes/wait-primitives.cpp (two threads, two 128-byte-aligned lines, a ping-pong of 2 000 000 round
trips; unpinned -- macOS pins nothing; Apple M4 Pro, Apple clang 21, -O2). ns per round trip = median over the launches
of a mode; "loads" = loads of the peer's line per round trip, each side. Two quiet sessions of the same host, the host
measured before each (hostguard: 93.9 % idle at 10:23 UTC; 92-94 % idle at 11:33-11:34 UTC, after the suites):

  mode      what sits between two loads of the peer's line                 10:25 UTC, 5 launches   11:34 UTC, 4 interleaved passes x 3 launches (medians)
  tight     nothing (the speculative poll)                                   72.0 (71.0-84.4), 133    85.7  71.1  71.9  71.5   (loads 136-143)
  ordered   `isb ; mrs cntvct_el0` -- qb's 3.3 pacing read at floor 0        67.4 (66.7-86.9),   4    84.3  85.9  84.5  88.0   (loads 5.4-5.7)
  yield     `yield` -- qb::spin_loop_pause() on arm64                        71.6 (69.8-75.6), 132    70.6  77.1  71.9  70.9   (loads 134-140)
  wfe       `ldaxr` on the line, then `wfe` (the exclusive monitor's wake)   70.3 (69.3-88.0), 1.1    73.6  70.2  73.6  71.5   (loads 1.1)

  The scratch build of the same source, two more passes at 11:35: ordered 85.5 / 84.9, tight 72.3 / 81.4, yield 71.0 /
  71.0, wfe 71.5 / 71.3 (rerun-interleaved.txt, rerun-scratch.txt, rerun-repo-probe.txt). The first session's 67.4 for
  the ordered read was not seen again: in six later passes of two binaries it read 84-88, the other three modes where
  they were (71-74, with single slow launches at 78-86).

  wfe with nobody storing to its line (2 000 samples, both sessions): min 41 ns, p10 1 291, median 1 292-1 333, p90
  1 292-1 334, max 2 584 ns -- the generic-timer event stream wakes it every ~1.3 us on this kernel.

Reading.
- `yield` is a no-op on Apple Silicon (132-140 loads a trip, the tight loop's round trip): qb::spin_loop_pause() paces
  nothing there, as QB-181 suspected; the flush backoff of VirtualCore uses it, the idle pass does not.
- The ordered counter read -- qb's pacing of the idle pass on arm64 since 3.3 -- is NOT the fastest form here: its
  round trip reads 84-88 ns in six of seven passes against 71-72 for the tight poll (+18 %), 67.4 once. The x86 finding
  of TUNING section 16.2 (a `lfence; rdtsc` between two polls issues the next poll late and once, and the round trip
  wins) does not transfer to arm64's `isb`, a full instruction-synchronisation barrier: 4-6 of them a round trip cost
  more than the late poll gains. What that means for qb's 2c-spin cells is measured on qb itself (`pacing-arm64/`: the
  tip against the same tree whose arm64 read drops the `isb`, three copies a side).
- `wfe` wakes on the peer's store at the tight loop's latency (70-74 ns, 1.1 loads: the first `ldaxr` after the wake
  sees the value) with the core asleep in between; without a store it sleeps 1.3 us, then re-checks. So on arm64 a
  spinning core could wait in `wfe` instead of polling, at no latency cost on the line it monitors -- and at up to
  1.3 us on any other producer's line, which is why a Mailbox with N-1 producer rings needs ONE line every producer
  writes (a doorbell: one more store per cross-core push) before `wfe` can carry the idle pass. What that buys is
  power, not latency; this host cannot count it (powermetrics needs root).
